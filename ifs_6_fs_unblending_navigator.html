Second Arrow Counseling Clinical Unblending Tool

IFS 6 Fs Unblending Navigator

A neurodiversity-affirming protocol for differentiating protective parts, verifying Self-energy, and assessing systemic fears without assuming standard somatic or visual processing styles.

Waiting Room 0 Parked

When a secondary reactive part emerges during unblending, park the active part here and pivot to the secondary protector.

No parts currently in the waiting room.

Step 1: Find

Objective: Target identification and spatial localization. Anchor the part somatically, spatially, or behaviorally.

Choose the anchor mode that fits best. Bodily signals, external space, and executive function hooks are equally valid.

If feeling blank or numb, treat "the blankness" as an active protector keeping things safe rather than an absence of parts.

Step 2: Focus

Objective: Attentional stabilization. Give dedicated internal time without trying to alter, fix, or analyze it.

Closing eyes is never required. Pick a sensory path that maintains gentle attention without overload.

Step 3: Flesh Out

Objective: Phenomenological profiling. Explore unique sensory qualities, distance, texture, pitch, or energy state.

If visual imagery is absent, focus entirely on auditory, tactile, or abstract conceptual qualities.

Step 4: Feel Toward

Objective: Diagnostic check ("Geiger counter") for Self-energy vs. secondary blended protectors.

Secondary Blended Protector Detected

You reported a reactive stance (e.g., frustration, fear, or urge to fix). This means a secondary protective part has blended to manage the target part.

Recommended Protocol: Direct address & pivot.

Step 5: Befriend

Objective: Establish trust. Investigate positive protective origin, historical burden, and current efficacy.

How old does this part think you are? Many protectors are stuck in historical time warps.

Step 6: Fear

Objective: Threat assessment. Identify catastrophic fears, guarded Exiles, and polarizations.
